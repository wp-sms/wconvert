# First-time campaign setup test — prepared, not run

14 September 2026. No participant findings or success rates have been collected.

## Decision

Can a first-time merchant choose a suitable setup, finish the required configuration,
and accurately explain what happens after a visitor submits or clicks?

## Participants and conditions

Recruit six first-time WConvert users: two store owners, two publishers, two service
business operators. This is an initial qualitative round, not a representative
survey or a statistical claim. Record their WordPress experience and current tools.
Use a disposable WordPress site with test destinations and a mail catcher; no real
customer data or public publication. Ask permission before recording. Allow 30–40
minutes and stop any task after 10 minutes without treating speed as product value.

Give each participant the two tasks closest to their work. Rotate task order.
Do not teach product terms, point at controls or name the expected setup. Ask them
to think aloud. If assistance is necessary, record exactly what was said and mark
the task assisted. Never pass off a guided completion as independent success.

## Task prompts

1. **Newsletter:** You publish a weekly article and want readers to request your
   newsletter. Set this up at the end of an article. First use the supplied test
   email service. Then imagine you cannot connect a service yet but want to save
   requests for manual processing. Show how you would do that. Before publication,
   explain what each choice does and does not send automatically.
2. **Download:** You have a PDF checklist to offer in exchange for an email address.
   Prepare the campaign using the supplied test file and delivery route. Explain
   where the file is configured, what the visitor sees, and whether an accepted
   email guarantees arrival or download.
3. **Enquiry:** You run a repair service and want visitors to request a callback or
   quote. Prepare the campaign for the service page. Explain who follows up, where
   requests are found, and whether the success screen confirms an appointment.
4. **Offer/content link:** You want visitors to see a sale page (store) or a related
   article (publisher). Prepare the campaign and test the supplied URL. Browse a
   different format, cancel, then apply an inline design and undo that change.
   Explain where the campaign appears and whether a click proves a purchase/read.

After each task, show illustrative counts: 40 submissions, 92 emails accepted for
sending, or 30 link clicks. Ask “What can you conclude? What can’t you conclude?”
Do not suggest the distinction first.

Record first choice, findability, misplaced confidence, missing settings, unclear

comments. Keep participant quotes separate from our interpretation. A confusion
is evidence of a problem; it is not evidence for a specific redesign by itself.

## Release decisions

Treat any critical false promise as a fix-before-release finding: believing local
collection sends messages, a request is a booking, a click is revenue, or browsing
silently publishes changes. Investigate repeated unassisted failures at the same
step even if the overall completion count looks acceptable. Prioritize severity,
not a manufactured aggregate score. Retest changed flows with new participants.
