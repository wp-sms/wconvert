Same foundation. A stronger promise.

Goal · why→Campaign setup · starting configuration→Design · reusable layout

Before this change

A Goal mainly selected starting points and grouped reporting. Some designs could contradict the Goal and still publish.

“Conversions” could conceal whether visitors submitted details or just clicked. Goal edits could relabel the entire history.

After this change

The Goal stays visible with its exact metric. Relevant designs appear first. Publishing checks the edited form or link—not just the original template name.

Drafts stay flexible. Published Goal history stays fixed, and another Goal starts a separate draft.

What users gain: fewer believable-but-wrong setups, clearer next steps, and numbers they can interpret without learning the data model.

The examples in this report use illustrative numbers, not actual customer results.

What users see along the way

Latest refinement: choices first, details on demand. Creation omits the redundant Campaigns heading band, and shared dialog headings no longer inherit excess spacing. Guidance is moved to the relevant decision—not removed from publication checks.

Store owner · “I want phone numbers for SMS offers”

The requested channel becomes a requirement.

Before

Choose “Grow my SMS list,” apply an email-only design, and publish. The counter may rise even though no phone numbers arrive.

The user has to discover the mismatch later.

After

Phone-capture designs appear first. Publishing requires a visible, required phone field on the form.

An email-only draft stays saved. The review says to add a required phone field or choose another design.

40 phone submissions

Means 40 submitted forms containing a phone number. It does not mean 40 unique people or 40 confirmed SMS subscribers. The connected SMS service owns subscription state.

No new consent or subscription-management system is introduced. Merchant copy and the connected service still need to match the intended use.

Service business · “Let people ask for a consultation”

A request is captured—not assumed from a click.

Before

The consultation starting point used a click-through offer design under Collect enquiries. The visitor could follow a booking link without leaving any enquiry in WConvert.

After

“Request a consultation” presents a contact form. The visitor supplies details and sees a request acknowledgement.

The merchant reviews Leads or receives the handoff through a connected service, then arranges the consultation.

12 enquiries captured ≠ 12 appointments

A captured request can lead to scheduling, but the form neither reserves a time nor proves a completed booking.

If the merchant wants a booking-page button instead: use an offer-click Goal and measure clicks. That remains a valid use case; it is a different claim.

Publisher · “Send my guide after signup”

Check the handoff. Name the evidence honestly.

Before

“Deliveries” suggested more certainty than the mail service provides. A form could publish without a selected, complete lead-magnet email Destination.

After

Publication requires a required email field and a selected, available lead-magnet email Destination with its required settings completed.

The headline says “Emails accepted for sending,” with an explicit inbox/download limitation.

100 submissions · 92 emails accepted for sending

This does not prove 92 inbox deliveries or eight failed recipients. Counts are events on their own dates; delayed work, multiple routes and resends can affect the comparison. Review forwarding for actual errors.

Publishing checks configuration, not end-to-end delivery. Test the real destination separately. No test email was sent as part of this implementation verification.

Merchant · “Now I want to promote an offer”

New intent gets new history.

Before

Change the Goal on a published campaign. Its old counters are interpreted under the new Goal, which can make the same 100 events appear to mean something different.

After

Before first publication: Change goal saves the current draft.

After first publication: Duplicate for another goal copies current draft edits into a new, unpublished campaign. The original and its results remain unchanged—even if it is currently unpublished.

Original · 100 email submissions→Original stays at 100+New offer draft · 0 results

The copied design still needs to fit the new Goal before publishing. Nothing automatically goes live. Draft Undo does not reverse a saved Goal change or alter another campaign's history.

What becomes different in everyday use

Before

“The Sunday letter” requires guessing the scenario. A newsletter may collect addresses with no forwarding configured. The gallery stays in its original format.

After

“Newsletter signup while reading” states the job. Choose Use this setup directly, or open Setup details for the checklist. In the editor, connect an email service or deliberately choose Collect only. The review names the choice and blocks an incomplete connected setup.

Choose Format → Inline, inspect a design, then Apply. Design and format change together. Cancel changes nothing; Undo restores both. Place the block or shortcode before launch.

Publisher example: “Recommend a related article” belongs under “Promote an offer or content.” Set a real article URL. Thirty link clicks means thirty clicks—not thirty completed reads or purchases.

WConvert campaigns are on-site forms and offers. Email/SMS campaigns in a connected service are a different thing. Campaign setups initialize a draft; Display rule sets change only the named display-rule sections.

Newsletter handoff example: With Send to a service selected and no destination checked, review says “No destination selected. Finish setup in Destinations.” The required fix appears first. If the merchant explicitly switches to Collect only, review instead explains local storage and no automatic sending. Selecting a route shows a count, not a claim of successful delivery.

Collect only affects the saved/published campaign configuration. It does not cancel forwarding already queued for earlier submissions.

Test understanding, not just completion.

Prepared — not run

Recruit six first-time users: two store owners, two publishers and two service businesses. Give each two relevant tasks in a disposable test site, without teaching the product terms.

  1. Newsletter: connect the test service, then explain and try Collect only.
  2. Download: configure a test guide and explain what email acceptance proves.
  3. Enquiry: request a callback/quote and explain who replies and where requests are found.
  4. Offer/content: set a real test link, browse a different format, cancel, apply and undo.

Record independent, assisted or incomplete outcomes, exact confusions, wrong turns and explanations of metrics. Any belief that Collect only sends messages, a request is a booking or a click proves revenue needs investigation before release.
